Questions

Do the translations need to be notarized?

No. USCIS asks for a full English translation and a translator’s certificate of accuracy and competence. Every translation comes with that certificate.

Can you translate only part of a long paper or patent?

USCIS asks for full translations and rejects summaries. If a document already has an English version, such as a US family patent or an English abstract, we’ll point it out so the attorney can decide what to file.

Are you a NACES member?

No. USCIS doesn’t require NACES membership. Degree evaluations here are written for USCIS petitions. For a state licensing board, use a NACES member.

Can the applicant order directly?

Yes. Applicants can order and pay, and the finished translation can go straight to their lawyer.

How do you keep technical terms right?

Names are fixed to the passport before work starts. Terms are checked against the field’s published English and the applicant’s own English papers. Where the source is ambiguous, you get the question before delivery, not a guess.

Do you take rush orders?

Delivery in 3 business days or fewer adds 50%. There are no 24-hour orders: every translation gets a full second read before it goes out.

Do you give legal advice?

No. Sen Linguistics translates and writes expert opinions. Decisions about the case stay with the attorney.
